## Findings

### F1 — `delta_t` is half the event half-width; the prose never states the factor 2

**DERIVED**, [`BREAK.md` line 34](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L34)
and [`surprise.py` lines 60–61](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/surprise.py#L60).

`d_t(h,t,a) = |h − Hc|/2` and `hband = (hc − 2·dt, hc + 2·dt)`. The event band is
`[125.200000, 131.491429]` (`BREAK.md:45`), whose half-width is `3.1457 = 2·delta_t`,
not `delta_t = 1.572857`. The factor 2 is the boundary slope `dHc/dMt = 2` in eq. 64, and
it is applied twice — once in `d_t` and again in `hband` — which is self-consistent with
the reported probabilities but not with a reader who takes `delta_t` as a half-width.
Such a reader reconstructs `[125.2, 128.35]` and gets half of every number in the table.

Recommended follow-up: state at `BREAK.md:34` that `delta_t` is the distance measured in
top-mass units (`|h−Hc|/2`), and that the event half-width in `h` is `2·delta_t`. No
number changes; the definition becomes readable. This matters more than it looks, because
the same factor 2 is what makes the one-sided/two-sided choice in F4 a factor of two.

### F2 — prior 2's support is not a mass range, and its p is a sliver of an unphysical one

**DERIVED**, [`BREAK.md` line 68](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L68)
and [`surprise.py` lines 26, 185](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/surprise.py#L185).

`flat_lambda_EW_0_4pi` is uniform on `(0, 4π)` and is mapped to `h` through the local
slope `dlambda_EW/dh = 0.00206`. That sends `lambda_EW = 4π` to `h ≈ 6164 GeV` and
`lambda_EW = 0` to `h ≈ 64 GeV`. The event sits at `h ≈ 128`, so the reported
`p = 0.001031` is `0.01296/12.566` — a 0.1% sliver of a range over which the linear fit
of eq. 55 does not hold and the electroweak vacuum does not exist as described.

`BREAK.md:113–114` concedes that the phase diagram over `lambda_EW = 0..4π` has not been
built, and `BREAK.md:133–136` concedes that much of `(0,4π)` would not stay perturbative
to `M_Pl`. Neither states the concrete consequence: this prior's support spans
`h ≈ 64–6164 GeV`, so its probability is not a statement about any physical mass range.
The direction of the error matters. This is the prior with the *smallest* p, i.e. the one
most favourable to the repo's floor (LEDGER S1), and it is the least defensible of the
four as a normalization.

Recommended follow-up: report the effective `h`-range of each prior's support beside its
p, so a reader can see which supports are mass ranges and which are not.

### F3 — the fourth prior's width is chosen with the datum visible, and it is the only one that flips the verdict

**INFERENCE**, [`BREAK.md` lines 70–87](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L70).

The prior set is 3-against-1: `0.008988`, `0.001031`, `0.045613` support "the proximity is
surprising"; `0.182451` supports "it is not". The single prior that crosses the issue's
0.1 threshold is the one whose width `0.05` is, by the document's own admission at
`BREAK.md:81–82`, "made with the data visible".

A physicist who did not know `lambda(M_Pl)` would not default to `|lambda| < 0.05`. A
default prior on a dimensionless quartic is `O(1)`, or the perturbative range, or
`(−1,1)`. A `±0.05` prior asserts that a small UV quartic is generic — which is the fact
under examination. `BREAK.md:72–79` defends it with "a physicist can entertain it," which
is weaker than "would have chosen it," and `BREAK.md:85–87` concedes it is "not an
objectively preferred ignorance prior." The concession is correct and should be read as
governing the 18.25%: it is a sensitivity case, not the default answer.

What survives the attack, and I checked it: the support is centred on zero rather than on
the observed `−0.0101`, and is about five times the observed magnitude
(`BREAK.md:73–75`), so it does not encode the sign or the exact value. The scale
`0.05 ≈ 5 × 0.0101` is still data-informed, so "not tuned to the answer" holds at the
level of sign and order of magnitude only.

### F4 — two-sided versus one-sided is decision-relevant and is not disclosed as such

**DERIVED, ran** (this session), [`BREAK.md` lines 39–41, 20, 100](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L39).

The event is two-sided, `|d| ≤ |d_obs|`, and `BREAK.md:39–41` discloses the choice. It is
a defensible choice for "closeness to a surface." It is not the source's convention:
Buttazzo et al. state the stability condition one-sided (`Mh > …`, eqs. 64–65) and quote
`2.8σ` as "99.8% C.L. **one-sided**" (p. 19, verified from the PDF). [STANDARD]

The choice changes the verdict, not just the number. One-sided means the metastable side
only, `h ∈ [125.2, 128.35]`, halving the band. Computed here for the fourth prior:

| event | scan `M_h` | scan `M_t` |
|---|---:|---:|
| two-sided | **0.182451** | 0.207617 |
| one-sided | **0.091226** | 0.103809 |

`p > 0.1` for the fourth prior (`BREAK.md:20, 100`) holds only for the two-sided `M_h`
event. One-sided in `M_h` gives `0.0912`, below the threshold; one-sided in `M_t` gives
`0.1038`, barely above. Across the four cells the threshold crossing is 0.091–0.208, i.e.
a coin-flip in the third digit. `BREAK.md` reports only the `0.182451` cell.

On the coordinate question: the boundary event is linear-invariant, so the band in
`lambda_UV` is the same whether the scan is in `M_h` or `M_t`; what changes p is the
support normalization and the slice direction, as the table shows. The `lambda_UV`-zero
event is a genuinely different event and is correctly kept separate
(`BREAK.md:55–56`). [DERIVED, ran]

Recommended follow-up: report the one-sided number beside the two-sided one wherever the
0.1 threshold is invoked, and state that the two-sided choice is the author's, not the
literature's.

### F5 — conditioning on `M_t` is not innocuous: a joint prior halves p

**DERIVED, ran** (this session), [`BREAK.md` lines 37–42, 116–117](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L37).

Every prior conditions on `M_t` and `α_s` and scans one coordinate. That turns the
boundary line into a 1D slice. For a uniform joint prior on `[0,700]²`, integrating the
2D band `|M_h − Hc(M_t)| ≤ d` gives area `2202.0` and `p_joint = 0.004494`; the fixed-`M_t`
slice on the same 700 support gives `0.008988`. The joint value is **exactly half**,
because the band spans only about `350` in `M_t` while the 1D support is `700`. The
conditioning therefore doubles p for a square prior; it is a choice with a factor-2 price,
not a neutral simplification.

A joint prior also asks the more honest question — joint proximity of `(M_h, M_t)` to the
critical line — and would count that `M_t` is itself near the edge (LEDGER D2), which the
fixed-`M_t` calculation deliberately drops. The perpendicular distance to the line is
`|M_h − Hc|/√5 = 1.4068`, against the horizontal `3.1457` and the vertical `1.5729`;
`BREAK.md:53` correctly says the slice is not a Euclidean normal. [DERIVED, ran]

Effect on the conclusion: the qualitative result — the number is prior-dependent and spans
0.001–0.18 — is untouched by any of this. The specific 18.25% and the threshold crossing
are not robust: the joint prior, the slice direction (F4), or one-sidedness (F4) each move
the fourth prior across 0.1. `BREAK.md:116–117` concedes that conditioning "relinquishes a
joint prediction of top and Higgs" but does not quantify it; the factor is 2.

### F6 — the S1 recommendation is slightly too strong at its load-bearing sentence

**DERIVED + INFERENCE**, [`BREAK.md` lines 18–20](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/edge/blob/1653b38/02-how-surprising/BREAK.md#L18).

The conditional structure is right: withdraw S1 *if* the steward accepts the small-quartic
prior, and `BREAK.md:85–87` concedes that prior is not objectively preferred. But
`BREAK.md:20` states flatly "The issue's p > 0.1 criterion is met there." It is met only
for the two-sided `M_h` event (`0.182451`); one-sided it is `0.091226` and the criterion
is **not** met (F4).

Given that three of four priors give `p < 0.05`, the conclusion the arithmetic supports is
not "withdraw S1" but "S1 is not compulsory under all defensible priors — it stands under
the broad priors and falls under the small-quartic prior, and which prior is defensible is
the steward's judgment." `BREAK.md:85–87` nearly says this; `:18–20` reads stronger than
the evidence. Net: too strong in the verb "withdraw" and in presenting the threshold as
met, and too weak in not stating that the other three priors leave the floor standing.

Recommended follow-up: phrase S1 as "no longer compulsory" rather than "withdrawn," and
carry the one-sided number beside the two-sided one. The steward's decision is unchanged in
kind; it is made on accurate numbers.
