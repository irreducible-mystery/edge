# The question, decomposed

**Why is the vacuum sitting on an edge?**

That sentence hides four separate questions. They are listed in the order they have to be
answered, because each one's answer changes whether the next is worth asking.

## Q0 — Is it on an edge? How close, and compared to what?
Not settled by "λ(M_Pl) is small." Small compared to what range? The honest version is a
number with a stated prior: *under prior P on the Higgs and top masses, the probability of
landing within δ of the stability boundary is p.* Different defensible priors give
different p, and the spread of p across priors is the real answer. Owner: `02-how-surprising/`.
Blocks everything below. If p is not small under any reasonable prior, the repo's floor
(LEDGER S1) comes out and the answer is "it isn't."

## Q1 — Is "three edges" one fact or three?
The quartic, the mass parameter, and the cosmological constant each sit near a critical
value. Candidate explanations differ on whether they explain one, two, or all three:
- asymptotic safety speaks to λ (and perhaps m²), not to Λ;
- multiple point criticality speaks to λ and Λ in its original form;
- anthropic selection speaks to Λ cleanly (Weinberg 1987), to m² arguably, to λ weakly;
- dynamical relaxation proposals are built per parameter.
A single mechanism that produces all three near-criticalities would be strongly favoured
over three separate stories. A table of which candidate covers which edge is the first
deliverable in `03-candidates/`.

## Q2 — What does each candidate predict that the others don't?
A candidate that only postdicts M_h = 125 is not yet distinguishable. Each file in
`03-candidates/` must end with: (a) one measurement or calculation that would kill it, and
(b) one that would kill its rivals and not it. Candidates that cannot produce (a) are moved
to the bottom and marked unfalsifiable-as-stated.

## Q3 — What kind of theory makes critical surfaces into attractors?
This is the question underneath. In statistical physics, criticality is usually either tuned
(a thermostat) or self-organized (sandpiles, where the dynamics drives the system to the
edge). The Standard Model has no thermostat. So either something outside it tuned it
(selection), or the dynamics of something above it self-organizes (asymptotic safety's fixed
point is one version; the Higgs scanning a landscape is another), or it is chance. This is
the question whose answer would be the floor under the Standard Model, if there is one.
It is OPEN and is the last thing to work on, not the first.

## Not in scope (for now)
- Deriving the dimensionless constants in general. Only the three Higgs-potential edges.
- Building a new theory. The job is to sort the existing candidates by what they predict
  and find the measurement or computation that separates them.
